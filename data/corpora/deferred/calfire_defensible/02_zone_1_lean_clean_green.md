# Defensible Space Zone 1 - the Lean, Clean, and Green Zone (5-30 feet)

Zone 1 covers from 5 feet out to 30 feet from any structure. The intent is to interrupt the fuel pathway from approaching wildfire to the home: any vegetation present must be discontinuous, low-growing, and well-maintained.

**Three tests of Zone 1 compliance.**

1. **Lean.** Vegetation is well-pruned, no dead branches, no dense undergrowth.
2. **Clean.** Free of dead leaves, dead grass, fallen needles, fallen branches, and other dead plant material on the ground.
3. **Green.** Living vegetation is hydrated and not stressed/dry.

**Specific maintenance requirements.**

- **Tree limbs at least 10 feet from the structure** (and at least 10 feet from any chimney). Trees adjacent to but not overhanging the home are acceptable when limbs cleared.
- **Tree canopies separated by at least 10 feet horizontally.** No continuous canopy across Zone 1 - embers can ride a continuous canopy into the home.
- **Tree limbs trimmed up at least 6 feet from the ground.** Eliminates the ladder fuel pathway from grass up into the canopy.
- **Shrubs spaced at least twice their mature height apart.** No continuous shrub bed; archipelago not contiguous.
- **No dead-or-dying vegetation, anywhere in Zone 1.** Remove on first sight.
- **Lawn / groundcover < 4 inches tall.** Mowed and watered; no dry stubble.

**Hardscape integration.** Driveways, paths, and patios within Zone 1 act as fuel breaks and are encouraged. Outdoor furniture should be metal or fire-rated; combustible storage (woodpiles, propane tanks > 5 gal) must be moved outside Zone 1 entirely.

**Insurance interaction.** Carriers writing in FHSZ Very High zones will frequently request photographic evidence of Zone 1 compliance for any premium discount. The most generous discounts are tied to BOTH Zone 0 and Zone 1 verified compliance; a few carriers extend a "Wildfire-Prepared Home" certified-property pathway with discounts up to 15%.

**Renewal cadence.** Zone 1 compliance is dynamic - vegetation grows, leaves fall. Annual maintenance plus a pre-fire-season inspection (typically May in Northern CA, June in Southern CA) is the standard rhythm. Some HOAs and master-planned communities now require documented annual inspection.

**Common Follow-up question.** "What if I cleared my entire 30-foot perimeter - would that drop my premium below the FAIR Plan + DIC route into the admitted market?" The Follow-up agent should retrieve this chunk + the FAIR Plan corpus, then explain that defensible space documentation alone usually isn't sufficient to swing routing - it needs to be paired with brush clearance, hardened siding/eaves, and ember-resistant venting before admitted appetite returns.
