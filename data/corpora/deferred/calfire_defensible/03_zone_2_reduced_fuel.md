# Defensible Space Zone 2 - the Reduced-Fuel Zone (30-100 feet)

Zone 2 covers 30 to 100 feet from any structure (or to the property line, whichever comes first). The intent shifts from "no continuous fuel" to "discontinuous fuel" - large vegetation may remain but must be thinned and limbed to prevent crown-fire propagation toward the home.

**Vertical and horizontal spacing rules.**

- **Tree canopies separated at least 10 ft horizontally** at the canopy outer edge. On steep slopes, increase to 20 ft or more.
- **Tree limbs trimmed up 6 to 15 feet from the ground**, depending on slope. Steeper slopes require more clearance because flames can travel further uphill before reaching canopy.
- **Shrub clusters spaced 2-6x mature height apart.** Larger spacing on steep slopes.
- **Grass mowed below 4 inches.** Cured grass during fire-season conditions can carry surface fire at 6+ feet/sec.

**Slope adjustment.** On slopes exceeding 20%, defensible-space distances are extended downhill: instead of 100 feet, treatment may extend 200 feet downslope. This is because uphill fire spread is faster and produces longer flames.

**What NOT to do.**

- Don't clear-cut. Total vegetation removal increases erosion, drought stress on adjacent properties, and (for invasive grasses) often makes fire risk worse, not better.
- Don't burn-pile in dry season. Use chippers or municipal greenwaste pickup.
- Don't remove riparian vegetation (creek/stream banks) without permit; CA Fish and Wildlife Code prohibits.
- Don't apply broad-spectrum herbicide near native habitat or watercourses.

**Ladder fuels.** "Ladder fuel" is vegetation that connects ground fire to the tree canopy: small trees, large shrubs, vines climbing trunks. The Zone 2 requirement to trim limbs 6-15 ft up specifically addresses ladder fuels. Insurers offering wildfire-mitigation discounts often weight ladder-fuel removal heavily because the surface-to-crown transition is the single biggest accelerator of structure ignition.

**Permits and HOAs.** Removing trees > 6 inches diameter at breast height usually requires a local jurisdictional permit, even when motivated by defensible space. HOA conditions, covenants, and restrictions may also limit removal; California Civil Code §4745 since 2018 generally allows defensible-space-compliant landscaping notwithstanding HOA aesthetics provisions, but enforcement is uneven.

**Wildfire-Prepared Home certification.** IBHS's Wildfire Prepared Home program is the most rigorous third-party certification: requires Zone 0, Zone 1, AND Zone 2 compliance plus structural hardening (Class A roof, ember-resistant vents, deck materials, siding). Some California carriers offer 5-15% discounts for certified properties; the program is rapidly being adopted as the industry standard for wildfire-resilience verification.
