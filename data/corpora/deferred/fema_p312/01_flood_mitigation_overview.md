# FEMA P-312 - flood-retrofit options for existing homes

FEMA Publication P-312, "Homeowner's Guide to Retrofitting", is the canonical public-domain reference for owner-occupied structural and non-structural flood mitigation. The guide describes six retrofit strategies with cost ranges, suitability matrices by structure type, and typical permit pathways.

**The six retrofit strategies.**

1. **Elevation.** Raising the dwelling above the Base Flood Elevation (BFE). Highest mitigation effectiveness; typical cost $150-$300/sqft of elevated footprint. Requires foundation rework; building must remain a single-family detached structure.
2. **Relocation.** Moving the structure outside the Special Flood Hazard Area (SFHA). Most expensive ($200-$400/sqft); reserved for high-value structures with available receiving lots.
3. **Demolition + reconstruction.** When elevation is impractical (e.g., slab-on-grade with structural compromise), demolition + new construction at compliant elevation may be more economical than retrofit.
4. **Dry floodproofing.** Sealing the building envelope below BFE so floodwater is excluded. NOT permitted for residential structures under NFIP because evacuation can be compromised; only commercial.
5. **Wet floodproofing.** Allowing floodwaters to enter and exit non-living areas (crawlspaces, attached garages) without damage. Lower cost ($10-$30/sqft); pairs well with elevation of HVAC and electrical.
6. **Levees and floodwalls.** Site-level perimeter mitigation; economical only for clusters of structures or when topography is favourable.

**ICC coverage.** NFIP's Standard Flood Insurance Policy includes Increased Cost of Compliance (ICC) coverage up to $30,000 for substantially-damaged structures (>50% of pre-loss value). ICC funds can be applied to elevation, relocation, demolition, or wet/dry floodproofing as appropriate.

**FMA grants.** FEMA's Flood Mitigation Assistance program funds 75% of cost for retrofits on insured structures that have flooded once or are repetitively-loss properties. Application is via state emergency management agencies; grant cycles open annually.
