# Elevation specifics - getting above Base Flood Elevation

Elevating an existing structure above the Base Flood Elevation (BFE) is the single most impactful retrofit a homeowner can pursue. The premium reduction is steep: NFIP rates for a structure 3 feet above BFE are typically 60-75% lower than at-BFE rates, and structures 4+ feet above BFE often qualify for the lowest rate class.

**Determining BFE.** The BFE for a property is found on the FEMA Flood Insurance Rate Map (FIRM) panel for the area. The FloodZoneTool in this system returns the panel ID, the flood-zone designation (X / AE / VE / etc.), and the BFE in feet (typically NAVD 88 datum) when available.

**Methods of elevation.**

- **Open foundation (piers, piles, posts).** Suitable for VE / V zones (coastal high-velocity wave action). Wave-rated piles or breakaway walls below the lowest horizontal structural member. Typical cost: $120-$200/sqft of footprint.
- **Solid wall foundation (extended crawlspace).** Suitable for AE zones (riverine / standing-water). Concrete or block walls extended to required height; mandatory flood vents (1 sq inch per sq ft of enclosed area). Typical cost: $90-$150/sqft.
- **Adding an upper story / using the upper story.** When the lower level can be converted to non-living use (storage, parking) below BFE. Less disruptive than full elevation; only works for two-story structures.

**Equipment elevation.** HVAC, electrical panels, water heaters, and similar equipment should be elevated above BFE even when the structure itself cannot be. Equipment-only elevation is inexpensive ($2,500-$10,000) and yields a meaningful NFIP premium reduction (10-15%) plus dramatic claim-frequency reduction.

**Inspection and certification.** Post-elevation, the homeowner must obtain an Elevation Certificate (FEMA Form 086-0-33) signed by a licensed surveyor or engineer. The certificate documents the actual lowest-floor elevation and is the basis for the new NFIP premium quote.

**Permit pathway.** Elevation is a substantial permit project: structural review, foundation engineering, often local floodplain ordinance compliance review. Average permit timeline 3-6 months; total project timeline 6-12 months.

**Common follow-up question.** "If I elevate my house 3 feet above BFE, how much does flood insurance drop?" The Follow-up agent should retrieve this corpus, anchor on the 60-75% premium reduction figure, and recommend the homeowner request a new NFIP quote with the post-elevation Elevation Certificate.
