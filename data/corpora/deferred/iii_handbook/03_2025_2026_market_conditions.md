# 2025-2026 homeowners market conditions

The current homeowners market (2025-2026) is shaped by three interlocking forces: catastrophe-loss trends, reinsurance pricing, and state-level rate regulation. Understanding these matters when interpreting this system's pricing outputs.

**Catastrophe trend.** US insured catastrophe losses averaged $94B/year over 2017-2024, vs. $54B over the prior 8-year window. Wildfire and severe convective storm (SCS) activity have increased the most; hurricane activity is volatile but not trending up in mean. The 2023 LA / Maui / Vegas / Hawaii cluster and 2024 Ian-Helene-Milton trio reinforced reinsurer caution into 2025-2026 cedeing rounds.

**Reinsurance.** Reinsurance pricing rose 30-50% during 2023 January renewals, then moderated 5-15% at 2024 January / 2025 January. 2026 renewals are tracking flat-to-down 5%. The market's view is that reinsurance has re-priced and is now adequately capitalized for normal CAT activity.

**State-level rate regulation - 2024-2026 highlights.**

- **California** approved its Sustainable Insurance Strategy in December 2023 - January 2024, permitting CAT modeling and net-of-reinsurance ratemaking starting 2024. Several major carriers (State Farm, Allstate, Liberty Mutual) returned to the wildfire-distressed market or expanded writings in exchange. Rate increases approved 22-35% statewide in 2024-2025.

- **Florida** passed SB 2A and SB 7052 in 2022-2023, addressing assignment-of-benefits abuse, AOB litigation, and one-way attorney-fee statutes. Citizens depopulation (transferring policies back to private carriers) accelerated 2024-2025 with $1.8M policies depopulated. Rates rose ~15-22% statewide; market now stabilising.

- **Louisiana, Texas, Colorado** all passed insurance market stabilization legislation 2023-2024 with varying success.

**Insurer-specific data.** State Farm announced March 2024 that it would pause new homeowners writings in CA wildfire counties; reversed October 2024 under SIS. Allstate similarly rebalanced. Citizens FL added 200K policies in 2023, depopulated 350K in 2024-2025.

**Outlook.** III's 2026 forecast: nationwide homeowners premium increase 8-14% in 2026, lower than 2024's 19% rise. CA and FL specifically: 12-18% in 2026. Higher-deductible adoption (5%-10% of CovA hurricane and 10%+ all-other-peril) is the leading consumer-side adaptation.

**Pricing Agent rationale.** When the Pricing Agent's ReWOO Solver composes the factor chain, the III Handbook's market-conditions corpus is what it cites for the "why is the rate at this level" prose. The Validator's cohort-benchmark check uses this corpus to challenge outliers - a Profile A at the 99th percentile of the cohort distribution but with a rationale grounded in 2024-2026 trade-press will survive Council scrutiny; one without won't.
