# Replacement cost value (RCV) vs. actual cash value (ACV)

Two settlement bases dominate homeowners losses: replacement cost value (RCV) and actual cash value (ACV). The choice materially changes the at-risk loss exposure and is one of the first decisions a Coverage Agent should surface.

**Replacement cost value (RCV).** Pays to repair or replace damaged property with materials of like kind and quality at current market prices, without deducting depreciation. RCV is the default for most HO-3 dwelling settlements when Coverage A is at least 80% of replacement cost. Required by Fannie Mae Selling Guide B7-3-02 and Freddie Mac §4703.2 for federally-backed mortgages.

**Actual cash value (ACV).** Pays RCV minus depreciation. For older roofs, ACV settlements can leave the homeowner with substantial out-of-pocket cost. Many carriers offer an ACV-at-loss endorsement on roofs over a certain age (typically 15+ years) at lower premium.

**Coinsurance / 80% rule.** When Coverage A drops below 80% of RCV, most policies impose a coinsurance penalty: settlement is the lesser of repair cost OR the ratio (Coverage A / 80% of RCV) × repair cost. The Coverage Agent should ensure the recommended Cov A floor satisfies the 80% test against current rebuild estimates from RSMeans / Marshall & Swift.

**Inflation guard.** Many carriers automatically increase Coverage A annually by an indexed amount (typically 3-7%) to track construction-cost inflation. After a major catastrophe (e.g., wildfire-driven labor cost spikes) the indexed amount may lag actual cost growth; insureds in CAT-exposed regions should review their replacement-cost estimate every 1-2 years.

**Settlement on outbuildings and contents.** Coverage B and Coverage C usually settle on RCV by default in modern HO-3 forms, but older policies may default to ACV; review the declarations page.
