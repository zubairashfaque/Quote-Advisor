# Catastrophic ground cover collapse vs. sinkhole loss in Florida

Fla. Stat. §627.706 governs subsidence coverage in Florida personal residential policies. Two distinct coverages are at issue: catastrophic ground cover collapse (CGCC), which is MANDATORY, and sinkhole loss, which is OPTIONAL with a statutory rejection-notice requirement.

**CGCC - mandatory inclusion.** Every personal lines residential policy issued in Florida must include CGCC coverage at no additional premium beyond the base homeowners rate. CGCC pays for damage caused by sudden and catastrophic geological events that meet four narrowly-defined criteria (per §627.706(2)(b)):

1. The abrupt collapse of the ground cover.
2. A depression in the ground cover clearly visible to the naked eye.
3. Structural damage to the building, including the foundation.
4. The insured structure being condemned and ordered vacated by the governmental agency authorized by law to issue such an order.

All four conditions must be met for a loss to be CGCC. The narrow definition is the load-bearing reason a separate (optional) sinkhole endorsement exists.

**Sinkhole loss - optional with statutory rejection notice.** Sinkhole loss coverage extends beyond CGCC to subsurface settlement that does NOT meet the four-condition CGCC definition. Insurers must offer sinkhole loss coverage to every applicant; rejection requires a 14-point statutory rejection notice signed by the insured (§627.706(2)(b)). The form text is dictated by statute and includes plain-language warnings about the coverage gap.

**Premium impact.** Sinkhole loss coverage typically adds 5-12% to the homeowners base premium, varying by county - Pasco, Hernando, Hillsborough, and Spring Hill counties carry the highest sinkhole-frequency exposure and the highest surcharges. The endorsement carries a separate sinkhole deductible (typically 10% of Coverage A).

**Inspection prerequisites.** Most Florida carriers require an engineer's sinkhole-activity inspection before binding sinkhole-loss coverage; the inspection cost (typically $500-$1,500) is borne by the applicant.

**Coverage Agent default.** For Florida applicants in counties without elevated sinkhole exposure, the Coverage Agent's Plan-and-Execute step 4 (endorsements) typically recommends DECLINING the optional sinkhole loss coverage and surfacing the 14-point notice; for elevated-exposure counties the recommendation flips to ACCEPT with rationale citing recent claim frequency.
